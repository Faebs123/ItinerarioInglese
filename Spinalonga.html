SPINALONGA ISLAND

Located in the Gulf of Elounda in northeastern Crete, Spinalonga Island is a place of profound emotional power. Known as the island of tears, it stands as a poignant monument to human resilience and historical memory.

A Bastion of Memory and Resilience

Spinalonga has a long and complex history, serving as a massive Venetian fortress and later an Ottoman settlement. Most famously, from 1903 to 1957, it became one of Europe's last active leper colonies, where hundreds of people built a self-sustaining community despite their exile.

Key Features

  • The Abandoned Village: Walk through the main street past the old shops, houses, and the hospital where the exiled community lived and worked.
  • Venetian Fortifications: Hike the complete perimeter of the island to explore the massive, well-preserved defensive walls dating back to the 16th century.
  • The Island Museum: Small exhibition spaces set up inside old stone houses displaying touching photographs and personal objects of the former residents.

Logistics & Tips

  • Timing: Visit early in the morning to experience the island's solemn atmosphere in quiet, before the large afternoon tour boats arrive.
  • Facilities: There is a small café and restrooms near the entrance dock, but no shaded spots along the walking paths.
  • Boat Access: Regularly reached by short, scenic boat rides from the nearby villages of Plaka (5-10 mins), Elounda (20 mins), or Agios Nikolaos (1 hour).

Note: The terrain is uneven, stony, and can become extremely hot in summer. Wearing sturdy walking shoes and bringing sun protection is highly recommended.
