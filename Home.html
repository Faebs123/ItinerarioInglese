History

Crete is home to the Minoans, Europe’s first advanced Bronze Age civilization. They built massive, complex palaces like Knossos around 2000 BC. The island’s strategic location made it a target for many powerful empires. After the Minoans, Mycenaean Greeks took control of the island’s major centers. The Romans later conquered Crete, turning it into a prosperous imperial province. During the Middle Ages, the Byzantine Empire and Andalusian Arabs fought for control. The Venetians ruled for centuries, leaving behind beautiful harbors and fortresses. In 1669, the Ottoman Empire seized the island after a legendary 21-year siege. Crete finally gained its independence and joined modern Greece in 1913. Today, ancient ruins and diverse architecture tell the story of this long history.

Culture

Cretan culture is defined by a strong sense of local pride and honor. The concept of Philoxenia makes hospitality a duty toward every stranger. Traditional music is led by the Lyra, a unique three-stringed instrument. Fast and energetic dances like the Pentozali are performed at festivals. Locals often express their feelings through Mantinades, which are rhyming poems. The Cretan diet is world-famous for its health benefits and longevity. Fresh olive oil, wild herbs, and barley rusks are essential daily ingredients. Traditional dress, including black shirts and boots, is still seen in villages. Raki is the traditional spirit served at almost every social gathering. Faith and family remain the core pillars of daily life on the island.

Curiosities
  • The Birthplace of Zeus: According to Greek mythology, the king of the gods was not born on Mount Olympus. He was born in a cave on Crete (either the Psychro Cave or the Idaean Cave) to hide from his father, Cronus.
  • The Labyrinth Legend: The huge and complex Palace of Knossos had over 1,300 rooms. It is believed that its confusing layout inspired the famous myth of the Labyrinth and the half-man, half-bull Minotaur.
  • A Forest of Olive Trees: Crete is home to an incredible 30 million olive trees. Some of these trees are thousands of years old and are protected as natural monuments, yet they still produce olives today.
  • Unique Wildlife: The island is the only place in the world where you can find the Kri-Kri. This is a rare, prehistoric wild goat that lives in the White Mountains and the Samaria Gorge.
