THE PALACE OF KNOSSOS

Located just south of Heraklion, the Palace of Knossos is the largest Bronze Age archaeological site on Crete and is considered Europe's oldest city. It stands as the monumental heart of the ancient Minoan civilization.

The Labyrinth of King Minos

Knossos is deeply intertwined with Greek mythology as the legendary home of King Minos and the mythical Labyrinth. According to legend, this vast, complex palace was designed by Daedalus to contain the fearsome Minotaur.

Key Features

  • The Throne Room: Home to the oldest throne in Europe, flanked by beautiful and well-preserved Griffin frescoes.
  • The Grand Staircase: A brilliant masterpiece of Minoan engineering, showcasing advanced multi-story architecture and light-well design.
  • Royal Apartments: Explore the Queen's Megaron, famous for its vibrant and iconic dolphin frescoes.

Logistics & Tips

  • Timing: Arrive early in the morning or late afternoon to secure shade, beat the heavy tour groups, and avoid the intense summer heat.
  • Facilities: A modern visitor center, gift shop, restrooms, and several cafes are available outside the main archaeological site.
  • Drive: Roughly 15 minutes by car or local bus from the center of Heraklion, with clear road signs and dedicated parking areas.

Note: The site is mostly open-air with very limited shade. It is highly recommended to bring a hat, sunscreen, and plenty of water.
